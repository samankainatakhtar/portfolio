Does the Model See the Disease or the Shortcut? Causal Lesion Grounding for Chest Radiograph Diagnosis

Ameer Tamoor Khan, Saman Kainat Akhter, Abdul Rehman Khan, Shuai Li

Medical AIChest radiographyShortcut learningCausal interventions

Chest radiograph classifiers can reach high discrimination while relying on cues unrelated to the pathology, such as burned-in markers, support devices, or site-specific image appearance; a correct label does not show that the lesion was used. We present RADAR, a lesion-grounded framework in which the disease probability is computed directly from class-specific lesion maps, so that diagnosis and localization share a spatial computation. Lesion extent is learned from multi-reader radiologist boxes, anatomical priors, and confidence-filtered teacher pseudo-labels, and controlled image interventions encourage lesion-specific prediction dependence during training: replacing the lesion with healthy tissue should remove the diagnosis, whereas equal-area off-lesion, outside-thorax, text-marker, and lesion-free edits should not.

On held-out images from three public collections with radiologist boxes and pixel masks, and on the external pixel-mask benchmark in particular, RADAR reached a macro area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.878, and at equal explanation area its lesion maps overlapped the radiologist masks more than gradient-based activation maps of a conventional classifier with the same encoder and training data (intersection-over-union 0.37 versus 0.32). After the annotated lesion was replaced with healthy tissue, 62% of the conventional classifier’s confident positive predictions persisted, compared with 16% for RADAR.

Replacing the lesion that RADAR marked lowered its disease probability by 0.78, against 0.29 for a matched off-lesion edit, and the gap persisted under an image operator never used in training. RADAR thus evaluates both localization and the sensitivity of diagnostic predictions to edits of the marked region, while acknowledging the limitations of synthetic image interventions.
